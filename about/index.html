About

George Willis

I work in spatial statistics, remote sensing, and applied machine learning. My core research investigates urban shrinkage across Chinese metropolitan regions: how cities lose population and industrial activity, and how that can be measured by combining twenty years of nighttime satellite imagery, point-of-interest kernel densities, and census returns using random forests and spatial econometric models.

Alongside that, I research web hyperlink graphs as an empirical proxy for inter-regional trade. Work published with Emmanouil Tranos and André Carrascal-Incera modelled trade flows between UK regions from web domain linkages: testing whether digital connectivity mirrors physical economic gravity models rather better than noisy regional survey data can capture.

There is usually a cleaner model underneath the mess. I'd rather build one tool that answers a concrete question than ten that just produce decorative choropleths.

I tend to approach problems on the assumption that there is a cleaner underlying model somewhere, whether it is an analytics pipeline, an urban growth model, an automated scraping engine, or why a transit catchment behaves oddly at borough boundaries. I enjoy the gap between "this is probably feasible" and "right, it now runs cleanly with tests, logs, and sensible configs". The projects kept on this site are the ones that answered something real.
