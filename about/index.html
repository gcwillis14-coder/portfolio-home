About

George Willis

I'm a geodata researcher working at the intersection of remote sensing, spatial statistics and machine learning. My core research looks at urban shrinkage in China — how cities lose population and activity, and what that looks like across satellite imagery, points-of-interest data and census records, combined through methods like kernel density estimation and random forests.

Alongside that, I've worked on using hyperlink networks between websites as a proxy for regional economic ties. Published work with Emmanouil Tranos and André Carrascal-Incera used links between UK regional web domains to model and predict inter-regional trade flows — an approach that started as a hunch about what "links to what" might reveal about who trades with whom.

I'd rather build one map that answers a real question than ten that just look good.

Most of what's on this site started as a way to answer a specific, sometimes small, question about a place — how far someone can get on a bus, how a city's lights changed over twenty years, whether two variables actually move together. The projects that stayed up are the ones still worth answering.
